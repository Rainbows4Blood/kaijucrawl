LOCATION TASK
Return {"location":string-or-null}. Use a concise readable string of at most 1024 characters. A single supported place stays a single string. If multiple places are genuinely covered, join the supported names using semicolons. A qualified name such as "Seattle, UCAS" or "Arkham, Massachusetts" is useful when both the place and qualification are supported. Do not invent political geography from memory: the same physical city can belong to a fictional state or have changed boundaries in the game's alternate history. Preserve fictional city names as well as real cities, countries, regions, neighborhoods, continents, buildings and other named locations. A specific fictional place outside Earth may also be preserved if the source actually situates its content there; the real-world date task can remain unknown.

Choose places where the adventure happens or that a sourcebook describes. Do not include a publisher's address, print location, designer's residence, convention venue, payment currency or a distributor's country. Named characters, organizations, corporations and game mechanics are not places unless the evidence clearly identifies a physical site. A corporation's headquarters qualifies when the site itself is part of the described action; the corporation name alone does not establish a city. Real-world publication language does not establish a country. A place mentioned only as an optional relocation, analogy or promotional comparison is not the book's primary geography. If the text genuinely presents several alternative playable locations, preserve those with a short qualifier instead of pretending one is definitive. Generic "a city", "a village", "the woods", "anywhere" or "worldwide" without identifiable geography is insufficient and yields null. An explicitly Earth-wide sourcebook may say "Earth"; do not assign Earth to every book simply because these games usually use Earth.

DATE TASK
Return {"date_start":date-or-null,"date_end":date-or-null,"precision":one-of-day-month-year-decade-range-era-unknown,"basis":one-of-explicit-modern-dark_ages-unknown}. Dates must be real Gregorian calendar dates in zero-padded YYYY-MM-DD form, years 0001 through 9999. Both bounds are inclusive. Use day precision only for an exact day; month for an entire named month, year for a whole year, decade for a whole numbered decade. Use range for explicit intervals, approximate intervals or one-sided dates. The normalized day bounds express coverage, not that the source supplied that exact day. Do not add a time of day or timezone. Keep chronological start <= end. Leap years and month lengths matter. A year covers January 1 through December 31. A decade such as the 1920s covers January 1, 1920 through December 31, 1929: its exclusive next-decade boundary is January 1, 1930, not an extra year of coverage. A stated span 1920–1930, in contrast, includes both named years unless the source says otherwise.

Read in-universe chronology, not the publication year, copyright year, release date, edition number, product ID, rating, price or real-world historical discussion unconnected to the scenario. A Shadowrun sixth-edition label does not imply 2080; a fifth-edition label does not imply 2075. The fact that a book is for Call of Cthulhu does not imply the 1920s. Locations alone do not establish time. A specific in-world year overrides a generic era label. If a description contains an explicit adventure period and a historical backstory, prefer the adventure period. Do not expand it back to a villain's birth date, an ancient artifact's creation, a newspaper quotation or a lore timeline mentioned in passing. If the book explicitly spans several periods or is an anthology of playable periods, range can cover their earliest and latest supported dates; do not add periods from comparisons or optional adaptation suggestions. If equally plausible dates contradict each other without a supported interpretation, use unknown rather than guessing.

For coc only, two fixed owner-approved fallbacks apply when the description establishes only a playable era without more specific dates:
- "Modern", "modern-day", "contemporary" or "present day" as the setting: basis modern, precision era, both date fields null. The application deterministically sets 1980-01-01 through 2030-12-31. Do not use the computer's current date or the publication date to alter this range.
- "Dark Ages" as the Call of Cthulhu setting: basis dark_ages, precision era, both date fields null. The application deterministically sets 0950-01-01 through 1050-12-31. Do not substitute a generic European medieval period.
These fallbacks are not allowed for Shadowrun. "Modern layout", "modern mechanics", "a contemporary author", "dark times" and "dark-age style art" do not establish a playable era. "Dark Ages" with an explicit year 1012 is that year, not the broad fallback. A CoC adventure in 1997 described as modern uses explicit 1997. No other era-only label receives a guessed numeric range. "Ancient", "medieval", "the future" and "long ago" without numeric evidence remain unknown. The two special fallback bases must have null bounds in your response because the application supplies their dates. If two different eras are both explicitly playable, use their combined supported range with basis explicit and precision range instead of choosing one at random.
