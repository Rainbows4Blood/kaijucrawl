LEVEL RANGE POLICY
The level range describes intended player-character levels for using or playing the material, not human player count, number of characters, monster challenge rating, spell level, number of pages, editions, dungeon floors, stages of a scenario, or the creator's experience. Extract a primary advertised range or explicit book-wide coverage. A single supported character level sets both min_level and max_level to that level. An explicit lower bound alone leaves max_level null; an explicit upper bound alone leaves min_level null. Unsupported bounds stay null independently. Never replace missing bounds with conventional D&D limits such as 1 and 20. Editions with epic or higher-level play can exceed 20; any nonnegative integer supported by the text is allowed, including explicitly stated level 0. Both known bounds must be in ascending order.

Prefer the main advertised range to optional adjustment advice. "Designed for levels 3–5; a GM could adapt it for level 10" is 3 through 5, not 3 through 10. A clear advertised "playable at levels 3–10 with included scaling" supports 3 through 10. A book explicitly presenting material for every character level 1–36 supports 1 through 36, even if the default rules you are familiar with end at 20. A generic rulebook without an advertised range does not imply any particular endpoints. "Any level", "low-level", "mid-level" and "high-level" without numbers leave both endpoints null. Do not derive numeric tiers from an edition label. Conflicting equally authoritative ranges without a clear main scope should leave uncertain endpoints null instead of averaging them.

For bundles and collections, use a stated overall supported range when present. A list of unrelated adventures with separate recommended levels does not by itself establish continuous book-wide coverage between the smallest and largest number. Do not combine independent entries into an invented interval. A concrete progression stated to take characters from level 1 to level 12 does establish that interval. Optional pregenerated character levels do not establish the intended range unless the description explicitly identifies them as the supported player-character levels. Keep a literal single bound when that is all the book establishes. Null is an analyzed unknown, not zero, unlimited levels, failure or not yet analyzed.

IN-UNIVERSE LOCATIONS POLICY
Return an array of place names that the text establishes as locations of the story, adventure, setting material, gazetteer or other substantive in-universe content. A place may be a world, plane, continent, country, realm, region, land, city, settlement, district, landmark, dungeon, building or other named geographical location. Include only names actually supported by the supplied text. Preserve useful spelling, accents and an explicit qualifier needed to distinguish a place. Trim surrounding whitespace and omit duplicates. If none can be inferred, return an empty array. Empty is a completed unknown/no-supported-location result, distinct from not analyzed.

Do not invent a hierarchy of ancestors. If the text only names a city, do not append its world and country from memory. If it explicitly names a city and its containing region, both may be included. A descriptive reference to "the city", "a nameless village", "the desert" or "a dungeon" is not a proper name to fabricate. A named fictional country may be inferred as a place even if the text does not state its administrative type. Do not list real-world author/publisher addresses, publication locations, web hosts, book stores, account names, creators, characters, deities, organizations, game systems, edition labels or titles merely because they are capitalized. Some names can denote either a person or place; include them only when this text uses them as a place.

Marketing comparisons, inspirations, lists of optional destinations, and incidental references unrelated to the book's actual content do not establish where the material is set. "Could be dropped into any city such as X or Y" alone is not substantive coverage of X or Y. A planar or spacefaring book does not automatically include every world it could reach. Do not infer a specific setting from D&D mechanics, prior classifications or recognizable art. Do not expand a campaign brand into remembered geography. Locations may be from an unlisted homebrew setting; they are not limited to the setting classifier's allowed choices. Use a list of at most 64 distinct names, each at most 160 characters. Do not output explanations, coordinates, type annotations or confidence scores as part of the names.

IN-UNIVERSE DATE POLICY
Return one string describing the in-world date, date range, era or chronology established by the text for the adventure, story or substantive setting material. Preserve the source's calendar terminology, year suffixes, named months, era names and qualifiers such as "circa" or "before". Different worlds use incompatible calendars; never convert to a real-world date or fabricate a calendar suffix. Keep numbers within this field as text. An explicit approximate date or named era is acceptable if it is anchored in the fictional chronology. A relative date tied to an explicitly named in-world event can remain a relative string; do not calculate an absolute year from memory.

Do not use publication dates, copyright years, revised rules years, sale dates, website timestamps, ISBN digits, character levels, ages of authors or the duration of a play session. A book's edition does not establish an in-world year. Nor does a setting name, recognizable title, narrator or historical allusion establish a date without text tying the material to it. Generic "long ago", "ancient times", "now" or "this year" without an identifiable in-world temporal anchor is insufficient and should be null. A calendar month or day explicitly presented as the story date may be retained without inventing its year.

Use the primary advertised story date or the explicit scope of a historical sourcebook. Mentioning an ancient event in background does not make that event's date the adventure's date. Where a book genuinely covers several explicit dates, preserve them in one readable string with their qualifiers or preserve its stated date range; do not average or silently choose one. If mutually inconsistent evidence cannot be resolved into a supported statement, use null. Do not add a setting or calendar interpretation from a previous analysis. Limit the string to 1024 characters, no control characters, and return null when unsupported. A null result is complete and should not be treated as a model refusal.
